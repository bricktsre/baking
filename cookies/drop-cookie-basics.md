Drop cookies are cookies that are "dropped" onto a baking sheet. They do not require any rolling, cutting, or any other speical shaping. Includes classics like chocolate chip, oatmeal raisin, snickerdoodle and many more!

### In Defense of Measuring (Weight, Time, Temperature, etc)
Many people look at measuring, specifically measuring by weight, as an unnecessary burden that slows them down and is not needed to make good cookies. It is certainly true that good cookies can be made by haphazardly throwing together ingredients and baking by feel. To me that is more effort and error prone than dialing in a recipe with exact numbers. Unlike other baked goods, cookies do not generally care about ambient air temperature, humidity, or exact moisture content. If the ingredients are measured properly and baked for the correct time at the right temperature, they will turn out the exact same batch after batch with no thinking required. Then you are able to experiment: turn the oven up, cooking time down, spin the baking sheet halfway through, less sugar, more flour for thicker cookies, the possibilities are endless. This will help you improve a recipe that did not turn out great the first time or prevent a random bad batch of your usually amazing cookies. 
I would advise doing the following:
- Weigh as many ingredients as possible. This is less important for low weight items like salt, baking soda, baking powder, vanilla
- Use an oven thermometer to see what temperature your oven actually is
- Take notes. What changed and what effect did that have?
    - Baking for shorter made the cookies softer
    - Adding more flour caused the cookies to spread less
    - A larger cookie was more delicious

### Temperature Management
One of the biggest factors in making good drop cookies is temperature management. This runs from the temperature of the ingredients to the temperature of the oven. Keeping the dough temperature at the right level will lead to better and thicker cookies
- The temperature of the eggs, butter, and dairy should almost always match. Most cookies want room temperature ingredients
- Chill the dough for 1-2 hours after mixing before scooping into balls, this makes scooping much easier
- After scooping chill the dough for 24-48 hours in an airtight container. This helps the fat to solidify and flour to fully hydrate resulting in thicker, tastier, and better texture cookies. If time does not permit this, try to chill another 2-3 hours after scooping for a greater than four hours total chill
- Go straight from the refrigerator to the oven. For cookies that need to be rolled in a coating try to put the first batch in the fridge/freezer while rolling the rest and always put the rest in the fridge once coated
- Only use a room temperature or cooler baking sheet. Placing it in the fridge can speed up cooling a hot sheet. Some people like to only bake with a sheet out of the fridge or freezer
- Frozen cookie dough balls can go straight into the oven with about a minute of extra bake time. Always chill in the fridge for 24 hours before freezing cookie dough balls
- Most drop cookies want a 5 minute rest on the baking sheet before being transferred to a wire rack to fully cool

### General Drop Cookie Advice
- Use a cookie scoop for uniform cookies that bake easily. The most versatile are #40 and #60 scoops
- Use parchment paper or silicone mats on baking sheets
- With a non-convection oven try to only bake one batch at a time on the middle rack. Be mindful of how large your baking sheet is since it may block airflow which is more important in a convection oven
- Drop cookies are usually done when the edge are set and lightly browned while the middle is still soft. For soft cookies the center may be softer than you expect. If there is a strong mismatch between edges and center try experimenting with bake time and oven temp
